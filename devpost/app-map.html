A three-stop route through the code

1. Follow the input

Open src/App.tsx and find extract. The announcement and selected timezone go into parseAnnouncement. Its returned deadline, notes and requirement labels become the editable draft.

Visible result: the “Your submission, decoded” panel. A missing date keeps Save disabled until a manual correction is valid.

2. Inspect the important decision

Open src/lib/parser.ts and find parseAnnouncement, REGIONAL and OFFSETS. Explicit EDT uses UTC−04:00; ET uses New York’s daylight-saving rules for the date. src/lib/time.ts contains wallTimeToISO and the reverse display/edit conversion.

The concrete evidence

The first case in parser.test.ts checks that Oct 26, 2026 at 5 PM EDT is 2026-10-26T21:00:00.000Z, displayed in Seoul as 2026-10-27T06:00. Separate tests cover seasonal PT/ET, invalid dates and missing-date input.

3. Trace the saved result

Back in App.tsx, find saveContest and persist. The verified draft becomes a contest. storage.ts validates saved records and uses the versioned key deadline-radar:v1. The list sorts by the saved instant. Countdown.tsx calculates remaining time and cleans up its timer.

Visible result: a tracked card that survives a refresh. Storage errors are displayed; they are never silently presented as successful persistence.

To change this, start here

  • Supported dates, timezone abbreviations or detected tasks: parser.ts → findDates, REGIONAL, OFFSETS, extractRequirements.
  • Urgency thresholds: time.ts → urgencyFor; its boundary test documents the behavior.
  • Checklist interactions: Checklist.tsx → Checklist.
  • Sample announcement and timezone menu: App.tsx → SAMPLE and ZONES.
  • Color, spacing and responsive layout: styles.css.

A practice to reuse

A specific expected result made timezone correctness testable. Compare prd.md → Announcement Extraction with the EDT-to-KST test. On another project, write one real input and its observable expected output before building the interface. Keep unresolved assumptions visible and provide a correction path.
